The short version

Four things worth knowing before you vote

01

Tomorrow decides the Norfolk DA, not November

Seven Democrats are running for an open district attorney seat and no Republican filed. The primary winner faces only independent Joe Pagliarulo in the general election. For practical purposes, Sharon picks its next chief prosecutor tomorrow, in the race furthest down the ballot.

02

The Democratic ballot has three contests; the Republican ballot has one

Democrats choose in the U.S. Senate race, the 4th Congressional District, and the Norfolk DA race. Every other Democratic office has a single name. Republicans choose only for governor, and seven offices on their ballot have no Republican candidate at all.

03

In both parties, the Boston Globe endorsed the candidate the polls have losing

The Globe backed Seth Moulton over Ed Markey and Brian Shortsleeve over Mike Minogue. Late-August polling has Markey up roughly 27 to 35 points and Minogue up about 27. The Globe also broke from the field in the DA race, backing Adam Deitch.

04

The generational challenger is losing young voters

Moulton's entire case is that at 80, Markey has held the seat too long. Emerson's final poll has voters under 40 backing Markey by 32 points, and women by 28. Whatever is moving this primary, it does not appear to be age.

How to read the comparisons

What the stance labels mean

Four labels, and the difference between them matters

  • SupportsThe candidate has publicly said they favor this.
  • OpposesThe candidate has publicly said they are against this. This label is used only where an actual objection is on the record.
  • QualifiedThe candidate supports the idea with stated limits, or backs a different mechanism toward the same end.
  • Not statedNo public position was found in the sources reviewed. This is not opposition, and it is not a criticism. Questionnaires and interviews put different questions to different candidates, so silence here usually reflects what a candidate was asked rather than what they believe.

Where one candidate campaigns on a position, the tables below give every other candidate in that race a cell on the same row, so the contrast is visible rather than implied. Coverage is uneven and the uneven part is stated: in the DA race, all seven sat for a WBZ interview, five have a published policy questionnaire, and four appeared at the Boston College Law School forum.

Race 1 of 4 · Democratic ballot · decides the office

Norfolk County District Attorney

Michael Morrissey is retiring after 16 years, and the office he leaves is under sustained public scrutiny over two cases: the prosecution of Karen Read, who was acquitted of murder in 2025 and convicted of driving under the influence, and the 2021 death of Sandra Birchmore in Canton, ruled a suicide by the state medical examiner and now the subject of a federal murder prosecution of a former Stoughton police officer. A third thread runs through both: the racist, sexist and antisemitic text messages of fired state trooper Michael Proctor.

Seven Democrats are running. The combined field has raised roughly $975,000 and spent about $489,000 as of early August. All seven say the murder charge against Read was wrong. They divide on nearly everything after that.

Race 4 of 4 · Republican ballot

Governor

The only contest on Sharon's Republican ballot. Not on this ballot

Nine questions waiting in November

Tomorrow is candidates only. The 2026-11-03 general election carries nine statewide ballot questions, several of which would reshape how elections themselves work. They are listed here because they shape what a vote tomorrow is worth.

Q1

Public records. Apply public records requirements to the Legislature and the Governor's office.

Q2

Public defenders. Permit collective bargaining for Committee for Public Counsel Services employees.

Q3

Top-two primaries. Replace the party primary system this election runs on with a single all-candidate primary.

Q4

Same-day registration. Permit voters to register and vote on the same day.

Q5

Tax revenue limit. Change the state's tax revenue cap.

Q6

Nature for All Fund. Establish a dedicated conservation fund.

Q7

Lot sizes. Limit required minimum lot size for single-family homes.

Q8

Marijuana. Eliminate recreational sales while allowing limited possession.

Q9

Firearms. A referendum on the 2024 state gun law. DA candidate Macy Lee has already said she supports upholding it.

Worth sitting with. Question 3 would abolish the party primary structure that produced tomorrow's two separate ballots. If it passes, the election you are voting in tomorrow is close to the last of its kind.
Method

How this was assembled, and where it is thin

Ballot contents come from the Secretary of the Commonwealth's official 2026 Democratic and Republican state primary candidate lists, cross-checked against Ballotpedia's district-level ballot for a Sharon address. The two sources disagreed in one direction worth noting: Ballotpedia's district set omitted every Norfolk County office, including the district attorney race that this brief treats as the most consequential thing on the ballot. The Secretary's list is authoritative and is what the tables follow.

Candidate positions come from published policy questionnaires, recorded candidate interviews, forum coverage and campaign reporting, all listed below. Two limits are worth stating plainly. First, coverage is uneven: five of seven DA candidates have a published questionnaire, four appeared at the Boston College forum, and all seven sat for a WBZ interview, so the candidates who answered more questions naturally fill more cells. Second, "Not stated" means no public position was found in these sources. It is a statement about the record, not about the candidate. Where a candidate has actually taken a contrary position, the cell says "Opposes" and quotes it.

Campaign finance figures are as of early August 2026, from a Brookline.News analysis of Massachusetts Office of Campaign and Political Finance records. Polling is late August and, in a primary with turnout this low, should be read as a rough signal rather than a forecast.
